## Evidence review: what the reported 0.2778 does and does not establish

The number 0.2778 is an owner-site score claim for `h33-h33-2-b2` in GEMSDOE32, not an organizer-confirmed receipt in this checkout. The local README explicitly records **“0.2778 = CLAIM; best receipt: none.”** The repository's pinned lineage gives a reproducible construction: begin with the H19-5 corroborated potential-field skeleton, thin it using a 2.8-pixel dot-spacing rule, then remove dots at/within the 2-pixel existing-fault collar; the result is 37,654 unit dots. The raster is reproducibly rebuilt and SHA-pinned in the registry. The lineage records the public-site 0.2778 attribution, but that number itself remains unconfirmed by DrivenData.

The official competition page describes a distance-weighted Tversky metric with alpha 0.2, beta 0.8, and a 300 m triangular distance kernel. In that metric, a sparse, spatially coherent lineament raster can plausibly outperform a thick or redundant surface: a nearby prediction receives distance-weighted credit, while prediction mass away from any label contributes false-positive cost. Thinning can remove redundant mass, and a catalogue collar can avoid emitting known mapped faults. This is a mechanism-based explanation, not a causal score decomposition. The site-reported comparison alone does not prove thinning or pruning caused the reported score; a matched ablation and organizer receipt would be needed.

No claim is made that the h33 result is the competition's best current score. The task text contains inconsistent current-leader values (0.3195 and 0.4413), while a static project-page snapshot is not the organizer ledger. These values need dated official receipts or a dated official leaderboard snapshot before they can be used as targets. Do not label a projection or a third-party page claim as a score.

### Why a sparse binary raster is a plausible fit, not a guarantee

The official metric rewards confidence values in [0,1] and uses distance to temper rasterization/misalignment error. A sparse 0/1 fault-line raster limits the total false-positive mass compared with assigning broad high confidence to uncertain terrain, while continuous line geometry may place predictions close to multiple target pixels. But a sparse raster can also under-cover unseen fault strands (false negatives); the metric's larger beta reflects that recall cost. Candidate density, topology, and spatial placement—not the mere use of zero outside or a unique filename—determine score. The official format page asks for null/NaN outside bounds; this repository contains zero-outside twins for compatibility investigations, but the NaN-outside twin is the closer match to the published format contract. Neither convention by itself explains a score.
